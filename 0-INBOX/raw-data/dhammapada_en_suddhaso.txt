Dhammapada
1. All things are preceded by the mind, surpassed by the mind, created by the mind. If one speaks or acts with a corrupted mind, Then suffering follows, As a track follows a wheel.
2. All things are preceded by the mind, surpassed by the mind, created by the mind. If one speaks or acts with a pure mind, Then happiness follows, As a shadow that never leaves.
3. “They abused me, they hurt me, they defeated me, they robbed me!” For those who hold on to this hostility is never replaced by peace.
4. “They abused me, they hurt me, they defeated me, they robbed me!” For those who do not hold on to this hostility is replaced by peace.
5. Hostility is never pacified by means of hostility. Only by means of non-hostility is it pacified. This is an eternal truth.
6. Others do not know that we must be restrained. The wise ones who know this attain peace.
7. If one focuses on beauty, lacks sense-restraint, does not know the right amount when eating, is lazy, and lacks energy, Māra subdues such a person just as the wind subdues a weak tree.
8. If one does not focus on beauty, has good sense-restraint, knows the right amount when eating, has faith, and has persistent energy, Māra does not subdue such a person just as the wind does not subdue a stone mountain.
9. An impure person who dons a monastic robe without true self-control is not worthy of the monastic robe.
10. One who has expelled all impurity, is focused on moral conduct, and is committed to true self-control is worthy of the monastic robe.
11. One who thinks the non-essential is essential and who sees the essential as non-essential does not arrive at the essence; their conduct is based on incorrect attitudes.
12. One who knows the essential as essential and the non-essential as non-essential arrives at the essence; their conduct is based on correct attitudes.
13. Just as rain penetrates a poorly sealed house, in the same way, passion penetrates an undeveloped mind.
14. Just as rain does not penetrate a well-sealed house, in the same way, passion does not penetrate a well-developed mind.
15. One sorrows here, one sorrows afterwards, an evildoer sorrows both times. One sorrows and agonizes after seeing one's own defiled actions.
16. One rejoices here, one rejoices afterwards, one who has made merit rejoices both times. One rejoices and exults after seeing one's own pure actions.
17. Tormented here, tormented afterwards, an evildoer is tormented both times. One is tormented by the thought “I have done evil,” And one is tormented even more when one has arrived at a bad destiny.
18. One delights here, one delights afterwards, one who has made merit delights both times. One is delighted by the thought “I have made merit,” And one delights even more when one has arrived at a good destiny.
19. A negligent person who says much that is beneficial but does not act accordingly is like a cowherd who counts other people's cows, and is not part of the contemplative life.
20. Even if they say little that is beneficial, one who lives according to the Dhamma, having abandoned passion, hatred, and delusion, with accurate understanding and a well-liberated mind, not grasping either here or beyond, is part of the contemplative life.
21. Non-negligence is the path of the Deathless, negligence is the path of death. Those who are not negligent never die, those who are negligent are like the dead.
22. This is what the wise distinctly know about non-negligence. They rejoice in non-negligence, enjoying the territory of the noble ones.
23. Those who meditate continuously, who always persevere, Steadfast, they touch Nibbāna—the unsurpassable safety.
24. Glory increases for those who are proactive and mindful, with pure and careful conduct, self-restrained, living the Dhamma, and not negligent.
25. By being proactive and not negligent, self-restrained and self-controlled, a wise person makes an island which no flood can submerge.
26. Fools and idiots commit to negligence, whereas a wise person protects non-negligence as the greatest wealth.
27. Do not commit to negligence or to involvement in sensuality; for one who meditates without negligence attains abundant bliss.
28. When a wise person dispels negligence by practicing non-negligence, then having ascended in the palace of wisdom, sorrowless, they look at this sorrowful generation, as a wise person on a mountain looks down at the fools on the ground.
29. Not negligent amidst the negligent, wide awake amidst the sleeping—like a swift horse leaving behind a feeble one, the wise leave them behind and move on.
30. By means of non-negligence Maghavā became the best of the angels; they praise non-negligence, whereas negligence is always criticized.
31. A monk who approves of non-negligence and sees the danger in negligence proceeds like a fire—burning up fetters, small and large.
32. A monk who approves of non-negligence and sees the danger in negligence cannot decline and is close to Nibbāna.
33. Unstable and unsteady is the mind, difficult to protect and restrain. The wise straighten it, like a fletcher straightens an arrow.
34. Like a fish pulled from its home and thrown on dry land, the mind thrashes about, struggling to escape the realm of Māra.
35. The mind is fast-moving and hard to subdue, landing wherever it wishes; it is good to train it—a trained mind brings happiness.
36. The mind is very subtle and hard to see, landing wherever it wishes; the wise protect it—a guarded mind brings happiness.
37. The mind travels far, wanders alone, incorporeal, self-sheltered; those who restrain it become free from the bonds of Māra.
38. For one whose mind is unsettled, who does not understand the true Dhamma, and whose clarity is disturbed, wisdom does not become complete.
39. One whose mind is free from influences, whose mind is unafflicted, who has abandoned both merit and evil, and is alert has no fear.
40. Having understood that this body is like a clay pot, having fortified the mind like a city, combat Māra using wisdom as one's weapon, and protect the victory—you can be free of any abode.
41. Before long, this body will lie on the ground discarded and devoid of consciousness like a useless log.
42. Compared to what might be done by an enemy to an enemy or one hostile person to another, a wrongly directed mind can cause even more harm than that.
43. Compared to what might be done by a mother or a father or any other relative, a rightly directed mind can bring even more benefit than that.
44. Who will investigate this earth, this world of death and angels? Who will collect the well-taught verses of truth, as a skilled florist collects flowers?
45. A trainee will investigate this earth, this world of death and angels. A trainee will collect the well-taught verses of truth, as a skilled florist collects flowers.
46. Having understood that this body is like foam, one who is aware that it is like a mirage, having cut Māra’s flower strands, goes where the Lord of Death does not see.
47. As if they were collecting flowers, a person collects mental attachments; like a sleeping village taken away by a great flood, they are taken away by death.
48. As if they were collecting flowers, a person collects mental attachments; never sated when it comes to sensuality, they are dominated by the Ender.
49. Just as a bee takes nectar from a flower without harming its beauty or fragrance—a sage behaves the same way when in a village.
50. Do not look at the faults of others—what they have or have not done; Look only at yourself—what you have or have not done.
51. Just like a lovely flower that has beauty but no fragrance, well-spoken words are fruitless when not acted out.
52. Just like a lovely flower that has both beauty and fragrance, well-spoken words are fruitful when acted out.
53. Just as one might make many garlands from a heap of flowers, in the same way, between birth and death one can do much that is wholesome.
54. The fragrance of a flower does not go against the wind; nor does the fragrance of sandalwood, milkwood, or jasmine. However, the fragrance of goodness does go against the wind; a good person diffuses it in all directions.
55. Sandalwood or milkwood, lotus or jasmine, compared to these kinds of fragrances, the fragrance of virtue is unsurpassed.
56. The fragrance of milkwood and sandalwood is insignificant; the fragrance of virtue is the highest—it drifts amidst the angels.
57. The path of those who have perfect virtue, live without negligence, and are liberated through right knowledge cannot be found by Māra.
58. Just as in a trash-heap discarded by the side of the road, a lotus might bloom—of pure fragrance and delightful to the mind—
59. In the same way, amidst the trash-heap of beings—the blind commoners—a disciple of the Completely Awakened One outshines them with wisdom.
60. Long is the night for one who is awake, long is a yojana for one who is weary, long is saṁsāra for fools who do not understand the true Dhamma.
61. If you do not find a traveler who is superior or equal to yourself, then resolutely travel alone; one cannot go with a fool.
62. A fool is tormented by thoughts of children and wealth; but since one does not even possess oneself, how could one possess children and wealth?
63. A fool who conceives of foolishness is wise in that regard; but a fool who has the conceit of wisdom can truly be called a fool.
64. Even if a fool spends a lifetime in the presence of the wise, they do not understand the Dhamma, just as a spoon does not understand the flavor of soup.
65. Even if a discerning person spends only a moment in the presence of the wise, they quickly understand the Dhamma, just as a tongue understands the flavor of soup.
66. Unwise fools act as if they are not even their own friends—they perform evil actions which yield bitter fruit.
67. It is not good to perform an action which one will later regret, when, weeping with a tearful face, one experiences its result.
68. But it is good to perform an action which one will not later regret, when, satisfied and uplifted, one experiences its result.
69. A fool thinks that evil is sweet as long as its results have yet to fully manifest; but when the results of that evil fully manifest, then they descend into suffering.
70. Month after month a fool might eat using the tip of a blade of grass; but such a one is not worth even a sixteenth as much as those who comprehend the Dhamma.
71. Once an evil action has been performed, it is not released suddenly like milk; burning, it follows a fool, like fire concealed by ash.
72. It brings no benefit when fame comes to a fool; it destroys their good traits and tears apart their head.
73. A fool wishes for a false existence—preeminence among monks, dominion in residences, and honor among other families.
74. “May both renunciants and laypeople think it was done by me alone; may I alone have the highest authority, in determining what should or should not be done.” In this way, the attitude, desire, and conceit of a fool grows.
75. Knowledge is the cause of gain, knowledge leads to Nibbāna; since this is exactly what a monastic disciple of the Buddha understands, they do not delight in acclaim; they devote themselves to seclusion.
76. Like a person who leads you to hidden treasure: this is how to see a person who points out your faults. A wise person who tells you what you need to subdue is the kind of person you should associate with. Associating with that kind of person is always for the better and never for the worse.
77. A person might advise you, instruct you, and keep you away from what is not good; They are liked by those who are good, and not liked by those who are not good.
78. Do not associate with evil friends, do not associate with lowly people; associate with good spiritual friends and with the highest of people.
79. When one enjoys the Dhamma, then one sleeps happily, with a clear mind; a wise person always appreciates the Dhamma proclaimed by the noble ones.
80. Irrigators guide water, fletchers shape arrows, carpenters shape wood, the wise train themselves.
81. Just as the wind does not move solid stone, praise and criticism do not disturb the wise.
82. Like a deep lake, clear and undisturbed, when the wise hear the Dhamma they attain clarity.
83. Good people let go of everything; the peaceful do not prattle about sensuality. Whether they are touched by pleasure or pain, neither elation nor depression can be seen in the wise.
84. Whether because of oneself or because of another, one should not wish to obtain children, wealth, lordship, or personal success in ways contrary to Dhamma. Instead let one be virtuous, wise, and in accord with Dhamma.
85. Few are the humans who go beyond. Most people only run around on the shore.
86. Those who act in accordance with the well-explained Dhamma will go beyond the mortal realm so difficult to escape.
87. Abandon dark things and develop bright things; a wise person leaves home for homelessness and lives in seclusion, where it is difficult to find satisfaction.
88. One who has nothing could hope to find satisfaction there, after abandoning sensuality; a wise person would cleanse themselves of mental defilements.
89. Those who have developed the factors of awakening correctly and thoroughly, who have taken up relinquishment, who appreciate non-grasping, and have eliminated their corruptions—such radiant beings have attained Nibbāna in this world.
90. In one who has completed the journey, is free of sorrow, has been completely liberated in every way, and has abandoned all ties, no fever can be found.
91. Mindfully they strive upwards, they do not appreciate settling; Like swans leaving behind a lake, they abandon home after home.
92. Those who have no stockpile, who have completely understood eating, whose domain is emptiness and objectless liberation—like birds in the sky, their movement is hard to follow.
93. One who has completely eliminated their influences and is not dependent on consumption, whose domain is emptiness and objectless liberation—like birds in the sky, their path is hard to follow.
94. One whose faculties are peaceful, like a horse well-trained by a charioteer, who has abandoned conceit and is free of influences, is envied even by the angels.
95. One who, like the earth, opposes nothing, who is as stable as a pillar, practicing well, who is like a lake with no trace of mud—such beings are not reborn in saṁsāra.
96. One whose mind is peaceful, whose speech and actions are peaceful, and who is liberated through right knowledge—such a person is at peace.
97. One who is beyond faith, knows the Unmade, has severed all ties, has expelled defilement, and has rejected longing—this is truly the best person.
98. Whether it is in a village or in the wilderness, in lowlands or highlands—any place where an arahant lives is a place worth appreciating.
99. It is possible to appreciate the wilderness areas that most people do not appreciate; one who is free of passion will appreciate them, since they do not seek sensuality.
100. Rather than a thousand sayings not connected to the goal, it is better to have just one sentence about the goal if one reaches peace after hearing it.
101. Rather than a thousand poems not connected to the goal, it is better to have just one line of a poem if one reaches peace after hearing it.
102. One might utter a hundred poems not connected to the goal; it is better to have just one line of Dhamma if one reaches peace after hearing it.
103. One might subjugate a million people in battle, but one who subjugates oneself has won the ultimate victory.
104. It is better to subjugate yourself than other people, for one who is self-trained always acts with restraint.
105. No angel or fairy, demon or god, can turn such a person’s victory into defeat.
106. Every month one might conduct a hundred sacrifices worth thousands; Or for a single moment one could pay homage to a self-developed person. That homage is better than a hundred years of sacrifices.
107. One might spend a hundred years tending to a fire in a forest; or for a single moment one could pay homage to a self-developed person. That homage is better than a hundred years of sacrifices.
108. One could make any kind of offering or sacrifice in the world for a year, pursuing merit; but all of that is not even a quarter as good as honoring those who are upright.
109. For one who is virtuous, respectful, and always reverential towards the elderly, four things increase: vitality, beauty, pleasure, and strength.
110. Better than living for a hundred years without virtue and concentration, is living for one day as a virtuous meditator.
111. Better than living for a hundred years without wisdom and concentration, is living for one day as a wise meditator.
112. Better than living for a hundred years with laziness and indolence, is living for one day with determination and resolve.
113. Better than living for a hundred years without seeing arising and vanishing, is living for one day while seeing arising and vanishing.
114. Better than living for a hundred years without seeing the path to the deathless, is living for one day while seeing the path to the deathless.
115. Better than living for a hundred years without seeing the ultimate truth, is living for one day while seeing the ultimate truth.
116. Rush to do good, keep the mind back from evil. One who is slow to do good appreciates evil.
117. If a person does something evil they should not do it again. Take no interest in it; the accumulation of evil is suffering.
118. If a person does something good they should do it again. Take an interest in it; the accumulation of merit is pleasant.
119. One sees evil as good as long as its results have yet to fully manifest; but when the results of that evil fully manifest, then one sees evil as evil.
120. One sees good as evil as long as its results have yet to fully manifest; but when the results of that good fully manifest, then one sees good as good.
121. Do not disregard evil, thinking it will have no consequence. Just as falling drops of water fill up a bucket, a fool becomes full of evil collecting it little by little.
122. Do not disregard merit, thinking it will have no consequence. Just as falling drops of water fill up a bucket, a sage becomes full of merit collecting it little by little.
123. Just as a wealthy merchant with little protection avoids a dangerous road, just as one who wishes to live avoids poison, one should avoid evil.
124. If a hand is unwounded then it can take away poison; poison cannot affect one who has no wound. No evil comes to one who does none.
125. If one abuses a faultless, pure, and flawless person, then evil will come back to that fool, like fine dust thrown against the wind.
126. Some people arise in wombs; evildoers, in hell; those on the right course go to heaven; the uncorrupted attain Nibbāna.
127. Not by going into the sky, or into the ocean, or into a mountain cleft, or anywhere in the world, can one find a place where one is free from one’s evil deeds.
128. Not by going into the sky, or into the ocean, or into a mountain cleft, or anywhere in the world, can one find a place where one is not caught by death.
129. Everyone dreads violence, everyone likes to live; recognizing that you are similar, do not harm or cause others to harm.
130. Everyone dreads violence, everyone likes to live; recognizing that you are similar, do not harm or cause others to harm.
131. Beings want pleasure; if one harms them with violence while seeking pleasure for oneself, one will find no pleasure in the future.
132. Beings want pleasure; if one does not harm them with violence while seeking pleasure for oneself, one will find pleasure in the future.
133. Say no harsh words to anyone; they might respond in kind. Hostile speech is painful, and it may provoke a violent response.
134. If you are non-reactive, like a broken bell, then you have reached Nibbāna and no conflict can be found in you.
135. Just as a cowherd, using a stick, herds cattle through a pasture, in the same way, decrepitude and death herd the living through life.
136. Performing evil actions, a fool does not attain awakening. The unwise person is tormented by those actions as if burned by fire.
137. One who is violent towards the nonviolent, one who harms the harmless, will soon experience one of ten consequences: 
138. Harsh sensations, deprivation, physical injury, severe illness, mental derangement, 
139. Legal trouble, severe accusations, loss of relatives, dissipation of wealth, 
140. Or their house catching on fire.Then, when the fool is separated from their body, they will reappear in hell.
141. Nudity, tangled hair, filth, fasting, sleeping on the ground, squatting in the mud - these cannot purify a mortal who has not yet gone beyond doubt.
142. However, if one who wears adornments is well-behaved, peaceful, restrained, committed to spiritual practice, and has renounced violence towards all beings, then they are a saint, a contemplative, a monk.
143. What person can be found in this world that is restrained by conscience? Who wakes up when criticized, like a fine horse struck by a whip?
144. Like a fine horse struck by a whip, be ardent and filled with urgency. By means of faith, virtue, determination, concentration, investigation of Dhamma, perfect knowledge, perfect conduct, and thorough awareness, abandon this immense suffering.
145. Irrigators guide water, fletchers shape arrows, carpenters shape wood, those who practice well train themselves.
146. Who can laugh, who can delight, since this is always on fire? Obscured by darkness, you do not search for light.
147. See that the mind has been made a puppet, damaged with arrogance, afflicted with excessive thought, lacking stability and composure.
148. This body is decrepit, fragile, a nest of disease, a putrid heap that falls apart, since life ends in death.
149. Set aside like gourds in autumn - when seeing these faded bones, what appreciation can there be?
150. Like a city made of bones, wrapped in meat and blood, a place of decay and death, conceit and contempt.
151. Even splendid royal chariots wear out; so too, this body decays. But the true Dhamma never decays: it is made known by those who are peaceful and virtuous.
152. A person who has learned little ages like an ox: their muscles grow but their wisdom does not.
153. Wandering through many lives, seeking but not finding the builder of this house - repeatedly experiencing the suffering of birth.
154. House-builder, you have been seen! You will build no house again. All your rafters are broken, the roof-peak dismantled. The mind has gone to the unconditioned - the elimination of craving has been reached.
155. Those who have neither lived a spiritual life nor acquired wealth during their youth, Waste away like old herons by a fishless pond.
156. Those who have neither lived a spiritual life nor acquired wealth during their youth, lay around like wasted arrows, lamenting the past.
157. If one values oneself, then one should guard oneself well; during any of the three phases of the night, a wise person should remain alert.
158. First one should settles oneself in what is proper, then one may instruct others; a wise person should not become defiled.
159. One should act the same way that one instructs others to act; one who is well-trained can train others - for it is truly hard to train oneself.
160. One is one’s own protector - how could another protect you? One who has trained oneself well has acquired a protector that is truly hard to acquire.
161. Evil is done by oneself, born from oneself, produced by oneself; in this way a fool self-destructs, like a diamond cutting through stone.
162. One who is excessively immoral, like a tree overgrown by vines, does to oneself what an enemy wants.
163. It is easy to do things that are worthless and of no benefit to oneself; but to do what is beneficial and worthwhile is extremely difficult.
164. A foolish person who, based on a harmful perspective, reviles the teachings of the Worthy One, brings about their own destruction like a monocarpic plant.
165. By oneself evil is done, by oneself one becomes defiled; by oneself evil is not done, by oneself one becomes purified; purity and impurity depend on oneself - no one can purify another.
166. One should not abandon one’s own welfare for the sake of others, no matter how many; having understood one’s own welfare, one should be intent upon it.
167. One should not participate in inferior teachings, one should not live negligently, one should not participate in wrong view, one should not increase worldliness.
168. One should apply effort, one should not be negligent, one should practice Dhamma well; one who practices the Dhamma rests happily, in this world and the next.
169. One should practice Dhamma well, one should not engage in misconduct; one who practices the Dhamma rests happily, in this world and the next.
170. Like seeing a bubble, like seeing a mirage - one who regards the world in this way is not seen by the Lord of Death.
171. Come, look at this world, as splendid as a royal chariot, where fools become immersed, but the conscious do not stick.
172. One who previously was negligent but now is not illuminates this world like the moon set free from a cloud.
173. One who replaces their evil deeds with wholesome ones illuminates this world like the moon set free from a cloud.
174. This world has become blind; rare are those who see clearly. Like a bird freed from a net, few go to heaven.
175. Like swans moving through the air, like mystics moving through the sky, the wise leave this world, having defeated Māra and his army.
176. There is no evil that cannot be done by one who tells lies and disregards the afterlife.
177. Stingy fools, those who do not praise giving, do not go the deva-world. The wise rejoice in giving, and because of that they are happy hereafter.
178. Compared to being the sole ruler of the earth, or going to heaven, or dominating the entire world, attaining stream-entry is better.
179. One whose victory does not diminish, whose victory cannot be removed by anyone in the world, the domain of that Awakened One is infinite - No one can lead that one astray.
180. One who is not entangled or stuck, in whom no craving can be found, the domain of that Awakened One is infinite - No one can lead that one astray.
181. They are steadfast, committed to Jhāna, appreciate the peacefulness of renunciation, and are envied even by the angels - those who are mindful and fully Awakened.
182. It is hard to become a human, it is hard to live a mortal life, it is hard to hear the true Dhamma, it is hard to encounter an Awakened One.
183. Not doing anything evil, commitment to the wholesome, and cleansing one's own mind - this is the teaching of the Awakened Ones.
184. Endurance is the highest austerity, Nibbāna is the highest, according to those who are Awakened. A renunciant does not harm others, a contemplative does not oppress others.
185. Not insulting, not harming, self-restraint in accordance with the Pātimokkha, Knowing the right amount when eating, sitting and sleeping in a secluded place, and commitment to improving the mind - this is the teaching of the Awakened Ones.
186. Not even with a rain of money can one be sated when it comes to sensuality. There is little gratification in sensuality, it is unsatisfying; this is what the wise understand.
187. Even in heavenly sensuality one cannot find satisfaction. But there is satisfaction in the elimination of craving for a disciple of the Fully Awakened One.
188. They go to many places for refuge - mountains, groves, forest shrines - people who are struck by fear.
189. But that is not a secure refuge, that is not the ultimate refuge; that is not a refuge which brings freedom from all suffering.
190. But one who has gone for refuge to the Buddha, Dhamma, and Sangha, and who sees the Four Noble Truths with correct wisdom - 
191. Suffering, the origin of suffering, the transcending of suffering, and the Noble Eightfold Path which leads to the pacification of suffering - 
192. This is a secure refuge, this is the ultimate refuge. This is the refuge which brings freedom from all suffering.
193. It is hard to find a high-quality person; they are not born everywhere. Wherever a steadfast person is born, their family grows in happiness.
194. Happy is the arising of Awakened Ones, happy is the teaching of the true Dhamma, happy is the harmony of the Sangha, happy is the austerity of those on the same Path.
195. One who honors those worthy of honor - Awakened Ones or their disciples, those who have transcended complication and gone beyond sorrow and grief, 
196. who have reached Nibbāna, and are fearless - it is not possible to calculate the merit of one who honors such beings.
197. Oh so happily we live, we who have nothing. We will feed on bliss, like the radiant angels.
198. Oh so happily we live, unafflicted amidst the afflicted; amidst afflicted people we live without affliction.
199. Oh so happily we live, unpreoccupied amidst the preoccupied; amidst preoccupied people we live without preoccupation.
200. Oh so happily we live, we who have nothing; we will feed on bliss, like the radiant angels.
201. Victory produces hostility; the defeated sleep uncomfortably. The peaceful sleep happily, having relinquished both victory and defeat.
202. There is no fire as strong as passion, no distress as strong as aversion, no suffering as strong as the components of being, no happiness greater than peace
203. Hunger is the highest affliction, conditions are the highest suffering; for one who truly knows this, Nibbāna is the highest happiness.
204. Freedom from illness is the highest acquisition, contentment is the highest wealth, trust is the highest kin, Nibbāna is the highest happiness.
205. Having tasted the flavor of seclusion and the flavor of peace, free from anguish, flawless, one tastes the bliss of the Dhamma.
206. It is good to see the Noble Ones, living with them is always pleasant. One who does not see fools will always be happy.
207. Those who go with fools will sorrow for a long time. Living with fools is painful, like being with an enemy all the time. But living with the wise is pleasant, like being together with one's relatives.
208. Therefore, follow one who is steadfast, wise, and educated, a good person who is patient, religious, noble, and astute, follow that person, as the moon follows the path of the stars.
209. One who is dedicated to that which is not worthy of dedication, and who is not dedicated to that which is worthy of dedication, has dropped the goal and grabbed their preferences - yet envies those who are intent upon self-development.
210. Don’t get caught up in what you prefer or in what you do not prefer. Not seeing what you prefer is hard to bear and so is seeing what you do not prefer.
211. Therefore don’t form any preference; loss of what one prefers is painful. One who has no attachments has no preferences.
212. Sorrow is born from preference, fear is born from preference; one who is free from preference has no sorrow and no fear.
213. Sorrow is born from affection, fear is born from affection; one who is free from affection has no sorrow and no fear.
214. Sorrow is born from partiality, fear is born from partiality; one who is free from partiality has no sorrow and no fear.
215. Sorrow is born from sensuality, fear is born from sensuality; one who is free from sensuality has no sorrow and no fear.
216. Sorrow is born from craving, fear is born from craving; one who is free from craving has no sorrow and no fear.
217. One who has perfect morality and wisdom, who is established in the Dhamma and understands truth, and who works on themself - this is the kind of person that the populace prefers.
218. Interested in the inexpressible, with an expanded mind, and not bound by sensuality - such a one is said be going upstream.
219. When a person who has been away for a long time arrives safely from afar, their relatives, friends, and loved ones delight in that arrival.
220. In the same way, when one who has made merit goes from this world to the next, they are received by their merit like the arrival of a beloved relative.
221. Abandon anger, discard conceit, completely transcend the fetter; Not adhering to mind and body, possessing nothing, one does not fall into suffering.
222. When anger arises, one who stops it like a runaway cart - That is the one I call a charioteer; other people just hold the reins.
223. By means of non-anger one defeats anger, by means of excellence one defeats non-excellence, by means of generosity one defeats stinginess, by means of truth one defeats lies.
224. Speak the truth, do not get angry, and give - even just a little bit - when asked; by means of these three things you will enter the presence of the angels.
225. Those sages who are harmless, who are always physically restrained, go to the undying state where they do not sorrow.
226. When one is always wakeful, training day and night, and intent upon Nibbāna, their defilements disappear.
227. This is an incomparably ancient truth, not just the way it is today: they criticize those who are silent, they criticize those who speak a lot, they criticize those who speak moderately; there is no one in the world who is not criticized.
228. It has not been, it will not be, and it is not now the case that anyone is exclusively criticized or exclusively praised.
229. The discerning praise one who, when observed day after day, is known to have impeccable conduct, intelligence, wisdom, morality, and concentration.
